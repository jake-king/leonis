# P4 — software-adapter render parity

Verdict: **fail against the SPEC bar (byte-identical), 2026-11-20.** Every adapter is deterministic with
itself, but no two adapters agree byte for byte — not even two versions of the same software renderer.
Every measured difference is at most **2/255 per channel**. Accepting a tolerance is a design decision
(SPEC, P4 row); the proposal is at the end, pending the operator's call.

Probe: `probes/p4-software-parity` (wgpu 30.0.2). Renders one 256x256 scene offscreen, 4x MSAA into
`Rgba8UnormSrgb`: a four-corner gradient, a fan of thin triangles, two overlapping 50% quads, a 4x4
checker magnified with bilinear filtering at a sub-pixel offset, and a rotated quad with sub-pixel
corners. Prints the SHA-256 of the raw RGBA. `compare.py` (stdlib) reports differing pixels, max channel
difference and differing 8x8 blocks; its `--selftest` plants a one-pixel, one-unit change and must
catch it (it does, locally and in CI).

The Linux lavapipe render from CI is kept as `p4/linux-lavapipe.png`.

## Findings

- **No cross-adapter byte equality, even for one renderer.** lavapipe changed pixels between Mesa 25.2
  and 26.2. A byte-exact baseline is only possible with a pinned renderer build.
- **The differences are rounding-sized and spread everywhere**: max 2/255, but in 80–99% of 8x8 blocks.
  A "fraction of blocks that differ" tolerance (what maps-tauri used) would call these images almost
  completely different, and a block-count tolerance loose enough to pass them would also pass real
  defects. Per-pixel max difference is the measure that separates rounding from a defect: the planted
  defect in compare.py's selftest is a 1-unit change, so a tolerance must be paired with a pinned
  baseline to catch defects that small.
- Each adapter is deterministic with itself (same hash on repeated runs, all three OSes).

## Proposal (needs the operator's decision; becomes ADR 0006 if accepted)

Two tiers for layer-3 snapshots:
1. **Exact tier.** Baselines are rendered by lavapipe at a pinned Mesa version inside the pinned CI
   container image (the same image `leonis ci local` uses, SPEC R15/R24). Comparison is byte-exact.
   This is where a 1-unit defect is caught.
2. **Cross-platform tier.** Every other adapter (WARP, Metal, mobile emulators, real GPUs) is compared
   against the exact-tier baseline with a per-channel tolerance of **max diff ≤ 2** and no block-count
   measure. Measured basis: this run. Raising it needs a new measurement on record (SPEC R6).

Upgrading the pinned Mesa version re-baselines the exact tier in its own commit, with the diff report.

## Not proven
- Text: the scene has no glyphs. Font rasterization is the likeliest source of larger differences;
  re-measure in M1 when text exists.
- Mobile emulators (SwiftShader on Android, the iOS simulator): P5.
- Real GPUs on macOS/Windows.
